# Tuning the hot shapes: 10.2% call-weighted saving, and upstream's MMQ J choice is wrong on two of the top five

## Notes

Measurements at /tmp/tune-prod.jsonl.measurements.jsonl on brutus (5.9 MB, 121 signatures). Analysis in /tmp/check.py and /tmp/weighted.py. Native baseline per signature is the fastest `*:native:*` candidate with status ok in that signature's own measurement set.

Tuning at `-c 229376` aborts: the tuner allocates reference-output buffers to validate candidates against native, and with `--cache-ram 0` at full context there is no VRAM headroom (`cudaMalloc failed` on 238 MiB). Tune at a smaller context and replay at production context — the hot shapes are ubatch-driven and unaffected.

## Findings

Follows RV18, which found the five hottest signatures had never been measured. Re-tuned on the RCCL build at the corrected production configuration (`--cache-ram 0`, `-c 32768`, `-sm tensor -ts 1,1`, draft-mtp n_max 5) driven by the **`full`** bench profile, which produces the width-512 prefill dispatches the `default` profile never generated.

**Coverage went from roughly 40% of calls to 98.8%** — 411,860 of 416,940 recorded calls, 121 signatures tuned.

### Call-weighted saving, the figure that matters

| winner family | native | tuned | saved | calls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **mmq** | 87,718 ms | 78,854 ms | **10.1%** | 298,284 |
| mmvq | 3,397 ms | 2,947 ms | 13.3% | 97,912 |
| blas | 401.5 ms | 401.4 ms | 0.0% | 15,024 |
| mmvf | 5.8 ms | 5.8 ms | 0.7% | 640 |
| **total** | **91,523 ms** | **82,208 ms** | **10.2%** | 411,860 |

**MMQ is 96% of matmul time**, not merely 73% of calls. Any work that does not move MMQ cannot move the total.

This supersedes RV11's 7.5%, which was unweighted and computed over a set that excluded the hot shapes entirely. The correct figure is 10.2%, and it is now derived from the actual execution distribution.

### Upstream's J selection is wrong on two of the top five shapes

| shape | calls | winner | vs native |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5120x512 . 5120x512 | 110,160 | `mmq:q8_0:j48:fb0:t256:o2:i128` | **+44.4%** (71.3 us against ~128) |
| 5120x24 . 5120x512 | 13,440 | `mmq:q8_0:j16:fb1:t128:o2:i64` | **+69.8%** (38.8 us against 128.3) |
| 3072x5120 . 3072x512 | 61,800 | `mmq:q8_0:j64:fb0:t256:o2:i128` | +2.7% |
| 5120x6144 . 5120x512 | 55,080 | `mmq:native` | 0 — upstream already optimal |
| 5120x8704 . 5120x512 | 11,960 | `mmq:native` | 0 — dead heat (685.85 against 685.97) |

Upstream's heuristic is well judged on three of the five and badly wrong on two. On the single hottest signature, a quarter of all matmul calls, `J=48` runs 71.3 us where upstream's choice takes about 128. The `5120x24` case is more extreme still — `J=16` with fallback at 38.8 us against 128.3 us, a 3.3x difference — and that shape's narrow N is exactly where a large J wastes most of its tile.

This is the clearest vindication of the whole approach so far: the wins are not in exotic generated geometry but in **choosing a different row of upstream's own config table**, per shape, on evidence.

## Conclusion

The answer to "can we improve MMQ for RDNA3" is yes, by about 10% of total matmul time, and the mechanism is J selection rather than new kernels.

Next, in order:

1. **Export this tune as a cache and verify replay end to end** on the RCCL build. These are the first winners measured against the real shape distribution, so this is the first cache worth deploying.
2. **Then measure end to end.** A 10.2% cut in matmul time is the input to the question RV11 could not answer. Whether it shows in tokens per second depends on matmul's share of wall time, which is still unmeasured — but the arithmetic is now far more favourable than the 7.5% over a minority slice.
3. **Make call-weighted coverage a routine report.** "121 signatures tuned" and "98.8% of calls covered" are different claims, and only the second one predicts whether tuning will matter. The join in /tmp/check.py should become part of `bigcherry report`.
4. **Reconsider the MMVQ geometry emphasis.** MMVQ saves 13.3% but on 3,397 ms against MMQ's 87,718 ms — so its entire contribution is 450 ms against MMQ's 8,864 ms. HI09's generated geometry is real and measurable, and it is roughly a twentieth of the available gain.

Note the run used `-c 32768` rather than the requested 64k: an earlier `sed` was lost when an ssh session self-terminated. Context does not affect the width-512 shapes, which are driven by ubatch, so the finding stands; a 64k confirmation is cheap if wanted.
