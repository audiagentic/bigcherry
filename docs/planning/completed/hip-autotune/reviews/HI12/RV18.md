# The tuner has been optimising 23% of calls: the five hottest signatures were never measured

## Notes

Analysis scripts /tmp/paths.py and /tmp/hot.py on brutus. Record at /tmp/rec-longctx.jsonl (121 signatures, 200k context); measurements at /tmp/tune-mtp-server.jsonl.measurements.jsonl (95 signatures, 16k context). The join key is the `signature` digest, which is shape-derived and therefore stable across the two runs; the composite `dispatch` digest is not comparable between them.

## Findings

Joining the long-context record (121 signatures, 416,940 matmul calls) against the tuning measurements, using the `signature` digest the tuner now emits. This join was impossible before today — result records carried only the composite dispatch digest.

**Which path upstream chooses, weighted by call count:**

| native choice | calls | signatures | share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mmq:native` | 303,324 | 36 | **72.8%** |
| `mmvq:native` | 97,912 | 49 | 23.5% |
| `blas:native` | 15,064 | 31 | 3.6% |
| `mmvf:native` | 640 | 5 | 0.2% |

**Hot signatures (standards 7.4 ranking), which nothing had produced until now:**

| calls | native | ne0 . ne1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 110,160 | mmq | 5120x512 . 5120x512 |
| 61,800 | mmq | 3072x5120 . 3072x512 |
| 55,080 | mmq | 5120x6144 . 5120x512 |
| 13,440 | mmq | 5120x24 . 5120x512 |
| 11,960 | mmq | 5120x8704 . 5120x512 |
| 11,212 | mmvq | 5120x8704 . 5120x**5** |

The top 12 of 121 signatures are **73.7% of all calls**.

**The finding: the five hottest signatures were never tuned.** 121 signatures recorded, 95 tuned, 81 overlapping, **40 recorded-but-untuned — and those 40 include the top five by call count, roughly 252,000 of 416,940 calls (60%)**.

All five are width 512, the ubatch, which is where MMQ runs during prefill and batch verification. Every tuning run in this project used a narrower bench profile than the recorded workload, so the tuner measured the MTP draft widths thoroughly and the dominant batch path not at all.

That reframes RV13 and RV11. The 7.5% aggregate saving was computed over the signatures that *were* tuned — predominantly the 23.5% of calls served by MMVQ. The 72.8% served by MMQ is unmeasured, so the aggregate is not an estimate of the achievable total; it is an estimate over a minority slice.

**The search space for the hot shapes exists and is compiled.** The RDNA3 MMQ config table defines twelve rows for q8_0 — J in {16,32,48,64,80,96,112,128}, each with fallback on and off, at nthreads 128/256 and I 64/128 — and the catalog carries matching `mmq:q8_0:jNN:...` candidates. None has been measured against a width-512 shape.

Where MMQ *was* measured, on a narrow shape, it was competitive: for `5120x8704 . 5120x5` the winning `mmvq:q8_0:w5:nw4:rpb4:sk1` ran 41.40 us against `mmq:q8_0:j16:fb0:t128:o2:i64` at 45.64 us — close enough that the outcome on a 512-wide shape is genuinely open.

## Conclusion

The tuning workload, not the tuning engine, is what has been wrong. Everything measured is sound; it was measured on the wrong distribution of shapes.

1. **Tune with a workload that produces the hot shapes.** Running now: the RCCL build at the corrected production configuration (`--cache-ram 0`, `-c 229376`) driven by the `full` bench profile, which produces the width-512 prefill shapes. This measures all twelve MMQ J variants against 72.8% of calls for the first time.
2. **Re-derive the aggregate saving afterwards.** RV11's 7.5% should be recomputed weighted by the call counts above rather than treating each signature equally. A saving on the 110,160-call signature is worth roughly 170 times one on a 640-call signature, and the current figure does not reflect that.
3. **Make coverage of hot signatures an explicit gate.** The natural check is exactly this join: recorded signatures ranked by calls, against tuned signatures. "95 signatures tuned, 0 failures" looked like success and concealed that the top five were absent. A tuning run should report what fraction of *calls*, not signatures, it covered.
4. **HI17 drops further in priority.** BLAS is 3.6% of calls and RV11 valued the forced-BLAS win at 0.5%. The leverage is MMQ at width 512.
