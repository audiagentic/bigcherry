# First valid end-to-end result: tuned dispatch is 1.7-4.4% faster on prefill, with no overlap between arms

## Notes

Script /tmp/ab2.sh on brutus; per-round logs /tmp/abb-{native,replay}-{1,2,3}.log; cache /tmp/prod.cache (121 winners, 7759 bytes); measurements preserved at artifacts/tuning-logs/mtp-prod-fullprofile.measurements.jsonl.

The `spec common_specu: backend offload failed; using CPU sampler` warning appears in both arms and is expected with tensor split, per the owner; it is not a confound.

## Findings

The end-to-end measurement RV11 could not obtain and RV15 invalidated, now taken under conditions that support a claim.

**Design.** Same binary throughout (`bc-build-replay-rccl`) with only `GGML_HIP_DISPATCH_MODE` differing, so there is no build variable and no RCCL confound. Three interleaved native/replay rounds so thermal and clock drift affect both arms equally, 20 s settle between arms, `-c 65536`, `--cache-ram 0`, dual XTX `-sm tensor -ts 1,1`, draft-mtp n_max 5, `mtp-dual` profile. Cache is the RV19 tune, the first measured against the real shape distribution.

| | native mean | replay mean | delta | rounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pp256 | 690.8 | **720.9** | **+4.4%** | 3/3, no overlap |
| pp1024 | 1029.5 | **1052.0** | **+2.2%** | 3/3, no overlap |
| pp4096 | 1253.9 | **1275.4** | **+1.7%** | 3/3, no overlap |
| tg128 | 116.8 | 117.0 | +0.1% | flat |
| tg512 | 78.7 | 82.7 | +5.1% | 2/3 |
| tg2048 | 86.1 | 88.7 | +3.1% | 2/3 |

**Prefill separates cleanly.** Replay: 720.7 / 724.7 / 717.2 against native 693.1 / 689.8 / 689.6 at pp256 — every replay run beats every native run at all three prefill points, with no overlap between the ranges. At n=3 that is about as strong as this design can give without many more repetitions.

**Generation is flat to noisy.** tg128 is unchanged; tg512 and tg2048 favour replay on average but with one reversed round each and spreads wide enough that the means are not trustworthy.

**Cache fully exercised:** 103,168 dispatched of 103,168 executed, 121 entries, **0 misses**, `stale: false`.

**The split is exactly what RV19 predicts.** The 10.2% call-weighted matmul saving is concentrated in MMQ at width 512, which is the prefill path — `J=48` at +44% on the hottest signature and `J=16:fb1` at +70% on another. Generation runs at narrow widths where the wins are MMVQ's, worth 450 ms against MMQ's 8,864 ms. So a prefill-visible, generation-invisible result is the coherent outcome, not a surprise.

That a 10.2% matmul saving yields 1.7–4.4% end to end also puts a first empirical bound on matmul's share of prefill wall time: roughly a sixth to a half depending on prompt length, with the largest relative gain at the shortest prompt where per-token overheads matter least.

## Conclusion

The dispatch layer delivers a real, reproducible end-to-end improvement on prefill. This is the first such result in the project; every previous attempt was confounded by a contended machine (RV11), a missing RCCL build (RV15), or winners tuned on shapes the workload does not execute (RV18).

What it is worth being precise about:

- **Prefill 1.7–4.4% is established.** Non-overlapping ranges across three interleaved rounds on one binary.
- **Generation is not established.** The means favour replay but individual rounds contradict, and tg128 is genuinely flat. Do not quote tg512/tg2048 without more repetitions.
- **The gain scales inversely with prompt length** (4.4% at pp256, 1.7% at pp4096), consistent with fixed per-launch selection benefits mattering more when there are fewer, smaller matmuls to amortise over.

Follow-ups:

1. **More repetitions on the generation arm** if a generation claim is wanted; five to ten rounds would settle tg512.
2. **Re-tune at the production context and re-verify**, since this cache came from a `-c 32768` tune while production runs 229376. RV16 showed a third of long-context signatures are absent at short context, so the deployed cache should be tuned against the long-context inventory even though the hot width-512 shapes are ubatch-driven and unaffected.
3. **This validates the whole chain end to end** — record, inventory, generate, tune, export, replay — on real hardware with a real workload, which is what HI12 and HI15 existed to demonstrate.
