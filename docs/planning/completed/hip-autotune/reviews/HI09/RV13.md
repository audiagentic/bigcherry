# Independent re-tune reproduces RV10 and RV11 on the real workload

## Notes

Cache exported to /tmp/mtp-portable.cache (6380 bytes, manifest d64e79c8). Throughput during the tuned run was pp512 503.74 and tg128 66.54, which says nothing useful — tuning overhead is present and the spread is ±10% (RV11).

## Findings

The portable-key change (HI23 interim) invalidated every measurement taken before it, so the real MTP workload was re-tuned from scratch. That makes this an **independent replication** of RV10 and RV11 rather than merely a repeat: same workload and settings, different build, fresh measurements.

Same configuration as RV10 — `llama-server` with `--spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 5`, Q8_0 27B MTP, 2× RX 7900 XTX with `-sm tensor -ts 1,1`, `-c 16384`, driven by the bench harness in `server-bench` mode, `SCREEN_SAMPLES=3 FINAL_SAMPLES=15`, `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1`.

| | RV10 / RV11 | Re-tune | 
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signatures tuned | 95 | 95 |
| Forced winners | 75 (79%) | 70 (74%) |
| Native winners | 20 | 25 |
| **Aggregate matmul saving** | **7.6%** | **7.5%** |
| Candidate status: ok / ineligible / tolerance | 1581 / 14550 / 136 | 1581 / 14550 / 136 |

The signature count, the eligible-candidate accounting and the aggregate saving reproduce almost exactly. The forced/native split moved by five signatures, which is the expected amount of churn for marginal decisions at 15 final samples.

Top winners, and the pattern is the same one:

```
mmvq:q8_0:w6:nw4:rpb4:sk1:v1   3 wins  22.46%
mmvq:q8_0:w5:nw4:rpb4:sk1:v1   4 wins  21.19%
mmvq:q8_0:w1:nw2:rpb2:sk1:v1   3 wins  17.46%
mmvq:q8_0:w3:nw4:rpb4:sk1:v1   2 wins  16.11%
mmvq:q8_0:w1:nw1:rpb2:sk0:v1   3 wins  15.12%
mmvq:q8_0:w4:nw2:rpb2:sk1:v1   4 wins  14.31%
```

**`small_k` dominates again.** Eight of the top twelve winners carry `sk1`, at the MTP draft widths 1–6. This is now the third independent workload on which `small_k` has been the standout dimension — the RDNA3 synthetic sweep (14 of 24 MMVQ wins), the original MTP tune, and this one. It remains the dimension `_variant_initialiser` silently dropped, so the registry could name it and never request it.

A slightly wider spread of `rpb2:sk1` geometries wins here than in RV10, which had `nw4:rpb4:sk1` more concentrated. Same family, same regime.

## Conclusion

RV10's finding holds on independent measurement: the dispatch layer is worth roughly **7.5% of matmul time** on the real workload, concentrated in `small_k` MMVQ geometries at MTP draft widths, with the best individual signatures at 21–22%.

RV11's conclusion is unchanged and now doubly supported: that 7.5% is well below what the end-to-end benchmark can resolve at ±10% spread, so the absence of a throughput change remains uninformative rather than negative.

The practical difference from RV10 is that **these winners persist**. The cache is keyed portably, so a catalog change or an upstream bump no longer discards them — which is what makes a 20-minute tuning run worth performing at all.

Worth noting for anyone comparing runs: the pre-change measurements are preserved at `artifacts/tuning-logs/mtp-s3f15-prekey-change.measurements.jsonl` and the new ones at `mtp-s3f15-portable.measurements.jsonl`. They are keyed differently and their `dispatch` digests are not comparable; the `signature` and `hardware` fields added by HI23 step 1 are what make the two runs comparable at all.
