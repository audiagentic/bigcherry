# Screening at 3 samples discards true winners: the same kernel measures 14% apart at 3 vs 15 samples

## Notes

Report at `docs/reference/TUNING-DETAIL.md`, regenerate with /tmp/report.py on brutus. Source data: /tmp/tune-prod.jsonl.measurements.jsonl (SCREEN=3) and /tmp/tune-screen15.jsonl.measurements.jsonl (SCREEN=15), joined against /tmp/rec-longctx.jsonl.

**Confirmed by experiment.** Re-tuning with `GGML_HIP_TUNE_SCREEN_SAMPLES=15` on signature `6608d41c`:

| candidate | median | samples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mmq:q8_0:j128:fb0:...` | **462.28 us** | 15 |
| `mmq:native:v1` | **464.92 us** | 15 |

They converge to within 0.6%, confirming they are the same kernel (`J_best = 128` for `ncols_max = 512`). At three samples `j128` had measured 531.40 us, a 14% inflation that eliminated it from final measurement. The prediction was exact.

**Aggregate impact is small, and this is the part to be careful about.** Call-weighted over the same 411,860 calls:

| | SCREEN=3 | SCREEN=15 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| total | 10.2% | 10.4% |
| mmq | 10.1% | 10.3% |
| mmvq | 13.3% | **12.7%** |

A 0.2 percentage point gain overall, with mmvq moving 0.6 points in the *opposite* direction. The two runs differ by less than their own run-to-run variation, so **the honest reading is that fixing screening did not measurably improve the outcome on this workload**. The candidates it was discarding were, in aggregate, ones that did not matter — including the case investigated, where the discarded candidate was native's own kernel and losing it cost nothing.

So: the mechanism is real and demonstrated, the reasoning it corrupts is real, and the performance it costs on this workload is approximately nothing. Worth fixing for confidence in per-signature results, not as a performance lever.

**Recommendation.** Measured screen error is roughly ±14% at three samples, while the documented retention band is "everything within 10% of best". The band is narrower than the error of the measurement selecting it, which is incoherent regardless of the aggregate impact. Either widen the retention band to about 20%, or raise screening samples to five to seven — the latter costs tuning time linearly and is the simpler argument to make. Full 15-sample screening is affordable here (121 signatures) but buys nothing measurable.

## Findings

From the detailed per-signature tuning report (`docs/reference/TUNING-DETAIL.md`), generated to answer whether the hot shapes can be tuned further. Three findings, one of them a probable defect.

### 1. Probable defect: fallback variants are never eligible

On signature `6608d41c` — `5120x6144 . 5120x512`, **55,080 calls, 13.2% of the workload** — `mmq:native` wins at 465.80 us and **every eligible forced candidate is slower**, the best being `j64:fb0` at 501.28 us (+7.6%). Both measured with the full 15 samples, so this is not a sampling artefact.

The candidate status list shows why that is suspicious: **every `fb1` variant is `ineligible`** — `j16:fb1`, `j32:fb1`, `j64:fb1`, `j128:fb1`. The same pattern holds on the other hot MMQ signatures. If upstream's own J search is selecting a *fallback* configuration for this shape, then `ggml_cuda_mmq_variant_is_eligible` is rejecting the configuration that actually wins, and the tuner can never propose it.

That would be the mirror image of RV02 and RV03: those were eligibility predicates that were too *loose* and admitted uncompilable candidates. This would be one too *strict*, silently excluding the winner. It is worth roughly 7% on 13.2% of calls, and possibly more elsewhere — `fb1` did win on signature `d4d829f5` (`j16:fb1`, +69.8%), so fallback variants are not always ineligible and are clearly valuable when admitted.

**Next step:** instrument which (J, fallback) upstream's `mul_mat_q_switch_J` actually selects for this shape, and compare against what `ggml_cuda_mmq_variant_is_eligible` admits. If they disagree, the predicate is the bug.

### 2. The J grid on RDNA3 is fixed by upstream and is coarse where it matters

The catalog contains `j8`, `j24` and `j40` candidates, all `architecture`-rejected here — they belong to other architectures' tables. RDNA3 defines exactly J in {16,32,48,64,80,96,112,128}.

On the hottest signature (`4dc5d204`, 110,160 calls, 26.4%) the optimum is **sharp**: `j48` at 71.28 us against `j64` at 90.24 (+26.6%) and `j32` at 90.64 (+27.2%). A narrow well between two sampled points usually means the grid is too coarse, so J=40 or J=56 could plausibly beat 71.28. Testing that requires adding `CASE` rows to `mmq-config-rdna3.cuh`, which is an upstream patch rather than a catalog change.

Contrast signature `035282e4` (61,800 calls), where the top four sit within 2.8% — a flat optimum with nothing further to extract.

### 3. MMVQ widths 6-8 are dead weight

Weighted by calls: w=5 51,488, w=4 28,236, w=1 7,594, w=2 7,050, w=3 2,020, **w=8 56, w=6 4**. With `--spec-draft-n-max 5`, acceptance concentrates at 4-5. Widths 6 through 8 are compiled and effectively never selected.

## Conclusion

**Corrected. The fallback-eligibility hypothesis in the findings above is wrong, and so was the follow-up guess that the forced-J path carries overhead. The real defect is in screening.**

### Fallback eligibility is correct

`ggml_hip_mmq_can_execute` derives fallback from the shape rather than treating it as a free choice:

```cpp
const bool shape_fallback = (sig.ne0[1] % 128) != 0;
if ((self->variant.fallback != 0) != shape_fallback) return false;
```

That mirrors `mul_mat_q_case`, which derives it the same way. For signature `6608d41c`, `ne01 = 6144` and `6144 % 128 == 0`, so fb0 is required and every fb1 candidate is **correctly** ineligible. On `d4d829f5`, `ne01 = 24` makes fb1 required — and `j16:fb1` duly won there by 69.8%. The predicate is right in both directions.

### The forced path is identical to native

```cpp
int J_best = mul_mat_q_compute_J_best<type, fallback>(args);
if (forced_J != 0) J_best = forced_J;
mul_mat_q_launch_forced_J<type, fallback>(ctx, args, stream, J_best);
```

Native (`forced_J == 0`) and a forced J reach the same launcher through the same code. Forcing `J = J_best` is not merely equivalent to native, it *is* native. So there is no forced-path overhead to explain the gap.

### What actually happened: screening noise

`mul_mat_q_compute_J_best` selects the J minimising `ntiles_x = ceil(ncols_max / J)`, which for `ncols_max = 512` is the largest eligible J, **J = 128**. So `mmq:native` and `mmq:q8_0:j128:fb0` are the same kernel on this signature.

They measured **465.80 us and 531.40 us** — 14% apart. The difference is the sample count: native was measured at **15 samples**, `j128` at **3**, because screening dropped it. Only three candidates survived screening to full sampling (native, j64, j112); the other eight were judged on three samples and never reconsidered.

So native did not beat the field. **The identical candidate was eliminated by noise in a three-sample screen**, and native won by default. Nothing was lost in this instance — the winner selected runs the same kernel — but the mechanism is general: any candidate whose true optimum is close to the leader can be screened out on three samples, and on this evidence a three-sample median carries roughly ±14% error.

That casts doubt on every screened-out candidate across the tuning run, including on signatures where the survivor is *not* secretly the same kernel.

### Fix under test

Re-tuning with `GGML_HIP_TUNE_SCREEN_SAMPLES=15`, matching the final count so screening cannot discard on noise. If the anomaly disappears — `j128` converging on native's time — that confirms the diagnosis. The cost is measuring every eligible candidate at full samples, which for 121 signatures is affordable and, given RV19's stakes, clearly worth it.

If confirmed, HI12's retention policy ("native always + top 3 by median + everything within 10% of best") needs revisiting: a 10% band is narrower than the measurement error of the screen that produces it.

### The other two findings stand

The J-grid observation (winners at 48 and 64, sharp well at j48 on the hottest signature) and the dead MMVQ widths 6-8 are unaffected by this correction.
