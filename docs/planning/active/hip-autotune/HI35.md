# Make tuning falsifiable — predicted matmul saving, and the decode fraction it applies to

## Description

`HANDOFF.md` states plainly that the current setup cannot answer whether tuning helps:

> matmul time falls ~7.5%, matmul is a fraction of decode wall time, and **the benchmark's own spread is ±10%**. The expected effect is about a third of one standard deviation. This setup cannot answer the question in either direction.

That is the single most important sentence in the project's documentation, and nothing has acted on it. Every subsequent tuning improvement — HI12's confidence testing, HI24's impact budget, HI34's fidelity work, HI25/HI26's new kernels — is currently unfalsifiable at the level anyone cares about, because there is no instrument that can see an effect of the size being produced.

HANDOFF names the three things needed. This item builds them:

1. **Measure matmul time directly rather than tokens/sec** — "the measurements JSONL already has everything needed".
2. **Establish what fraction of decode time is matmul at all**, "which nothing has measured and which caps everything".
3. **Only then decide** whether an end-to-end number is worth the 50+ repetitions it would need.

Item 1 is arithmetic over collected data and needs no GPU. Item 2 is one profiling run. Item 3 is a power calculation that follows from the first two. None of it is large; all of it is currently missing.

## Steps

1. **`bigcherry.impact`** — a tool that joins a record JSONL (calls per signature) to a measurements JSONL (median per candidate per signature) and reports predicted total matmul time under native winners against tuned winners, with per-family and per-signature breakdowns. This formalises the calculation RV19 did by hand and makes it reproducible against any pair of artifacts.
2. **Confidence intervals on the prediction**, not a point estimate. Once HI12's E2 lands, the raw samples are in the file; propagate them rather than multiplying medians and presenting the product as exact.
3. **Measure the matmul fraction of decode** with `rocprofv3` kernel tracing on the target configuration, aggregating kernel time by family. Reuse the mangled-symbol to stable-name mapping HI09b needs anyway.
4. **Compute the expected end-to-end effect** as `matmul_fraction x predicted_saving`, and the repetition count an A/B would need to resolve it at the observed benchmark spread.
5. **Decide and record**: run the A/B at that repetition count, or state in HANDOFF that the effect is below what this benchmark can resolve and stop quoting end-to-end numbers for it.
6. **Wire `bigcherry.impact` into the tuning workflow** so every tune reports its own predicted saving at the end, rather than that figure being reconstructed months later by whoever asks.

## Detailed Solution & Technical Design

### Part 1 — the predicted-saving model

#### What it computes

For each signature present in both artifacts:

```
saved_us(sig)      = calls(sig) x (native_median_us(sig) - winner_median_us(sig))
predicted_total_us = sum over sig of calls(sig) x winner_median_us(sig)
native_total_us    = sum over sig of calls(sig) x native_median_us(sig)
saving_pct         = 100 x (native_total - predicted_total) / native_total
```

Broken down by family and listed per signature, descending by `saved_us`. That
last ordering is the useful one and is different from every ranking the project
currently produces: it answers "where did the saving actually come from",
which is not the same question as "which signature is hottest" or "which winner
has the largest margin".

RV19 already established what the answer looks like — 10.2% call-weighted, with
MMQ at 96% of matmul time — but that was computed once, by hand, for one pair of
files. It cannot be recomputed after a re-tune without redoing the work, so in
practice nobody does.

#### Two things it must not hide

**Coverage.** A signature in the record but not the measurements was never
tuned, and a signature in the measurements but not the record was tuned for a
workload this record does not represent. Both are common and both change the
answer. Report them as explicit counts and as a share of calls — the Gemma E4B
case in HANDOFF (21 replay hits against 100 misses) is exactly this failure, and
an impact model that silently inner-joined would have reported a healthy saving
for a cache that barely executed.

**Uncertainty.** `winner_median_us` is a median over 15 samples with a MAD
beside it, and `calls` is exact. Multiplying two medians and printing four
significant figures overstates what is known. Once HI12's E2 puts raw samples in
the file, resample per signature to get an interval on the total. Before that,
at minimum propagate MAD as a crude band and label it as such.

#### Why this is the primary instrument and end-to-end is not

It has no measurement noise of its own. The calls are counted exactly, the
medians are measured under controlled interleaving, and the arithmetic is
deterministic. An end-to-end benchmark, by contrast, spends its resolution on
the 70-odd percent of decode time that tuning cannot touch. For an intervention
whose effect is a few percent of a minority of wall time, the model is the more
sensitive instrument by a wide margin — and it is also the one that says *which
signature* changed, which no end-to-end number can.

Its weakness is exactly complementary: it assumes per-call isolated timings
compose linearly into wall time. They do not, quite — cache state carries between
ops (HI34), and the CPU may be the limiter at draft widths (HI12's E3). Which is
why part 2 exists.

### Part 2 — the fraction nothing has measured

#### Why not an in-process timer

The obvious approach — wrap every dispatch in a host timer — is wrong, and
wrong in a way that produces a plausible number. Launches are asynchronous, so a
host timestamp pair around `ggml_hip_dispatch_launch` measures *submission*, not
execution. Wrapping each dispatch in a hipEvent pair instead measures execution
but serialises the stream, changing the thing being measured and inflating total
GPU time by removing overlap.

#### Use the profiler

`rocprofv3` kernel tracing gives per-kernel durations for a real run with no code
change and no serialisation. Aggregate by kernel symbol, map symbols to families,
and divide by wall time.

The symbol-to-family mapping is the only fiddly part, and HI09b already requires
it — that item parses `-Rpass-analysis=kernel-resource-usage` remarks and "maps
mangled kernel symbols back to stable names". Build the mapping once, in one
place, and let both items use it.

#### What the number is for

It is the ceiling. If matmul is 30% of decode GPU time, a 10% matmul saving is
at most 3% end to end, and that is before Amdahl's law meets the CPU-bound parts
of decode. If it turns out to be 60%, the case for more tuning work strengthens
sharply. Nobody currently knows which, and the entire prioritisation between
"tune better" (HI12/HI24/HI34) and "remove work" (HI26) rests on it.

Measure it separately for prefill and decode. The recorded numbers already say
they behave differently — prefill shows +1.7% to +4.4% while generation is flat
— and a single blended fraction would explain neither.

---

### Part 3 — what an end-to-end A/B would actually cost

With the effect size from parts 1 and 2 and the spread from the benchmark, the
required repetition count follows. For a two-sided test at alpha 0.05 and 80%
power, roughly `n = 16 x (s/d)^2` per arm.

Using the figures already in HANDOFF — tg128 at 67.99 ± 6.92 t/s, so
s = 10.2% — and assuming a matmul fraction of 30% against a 10% matmul saving,
d = 3%:

```
n = 16 x (10.2 / 3.0)^2 = 185 repetitions per arm
```

At 1% end-to-end effect it is over 1,600. HANDOFF's "50+ repetitions" is
optimistic; the honest figure is likely an order of magnitude worse, and that is
with an *assumed* fraction. This calculation is the deliverable — it converts an
open question into a cost, and a cost can be declined explicitly.

**Pairing is the one lever that helps.** Interleaving native and replay rounds
within one session, as the 2026-08-05 comparison did, cancels the machine-state
variance that dominates `s`. The same reasoning as HI12's E1, one level up. Any
A/B that does get run must be paired and interleaved, and its analysis must use
the pairing — not compare two means of independent runs.

## Code Samples & Guidance

### The format skew every tool here has to survive

The measurements files this item validates against **predate HI12's E-series**.
`tune9b.measurements.jsonl` and `tune4b-bench.measurements.jsonl` on disk carry:

```json
{"kind":"result","dispatch":"...","signature":"...","hardware":"...",
 "winner":"mmvq:native:v1","improvement_pct":0.000,"generated":270,
 "eligible":4,"measured":2,"reason":"native retained","canary_pct":-1.000,
 "canary_retries":0,"canary_pair":"","candidates":[...]}
```

No `canonical`, no `applicable`, no `confidence`, no `rejects`, no `native`; and
per candidate no `effective_us`, no `sign_p`, no `samples_us`. Since HI35's
headline validation is *"reproduces RV19: 10.2% call-weighted with MMQ at ~96%"*,
and RV19's artifacts are old-format, **`impact.py` must not require any field
added after them.** Concretely:

- the point estimate uses `median_us` only, present in both;
- the bootstrap interval requires `samples_us` and must **degrade to "interval
  unavailable (no raw samples in this file)"**, never to a silently narrower
  band computed from something else;
- native is resolved from `result["native"]` when present and from the record
  file's `observation.native` otherwise. Scanning for `*:native:v1` is not an
  acceptable third option — every old-format result carries `blas:native:v1`
  beside `mmq:native:v1`, and picking the wrong one re-bases the entire model.
  See HI24 for the one-line tuner change that removes the join.

```python
@dataclasses.dataclass
class Coverage:
    """What the join could and could not match.

    Reported, never quietly dropped. A signature recorded but never tuned
    contributes its full native cost and no saving; a signature tuned but never
    recorded contributes nothing and means the tune was run against a different
    workload. Both are common, both change the answer, and an inner join hides
    both -- which is how a cache with 21 hits and 100 misses could still have
    reported a healthy saving over the 21.
    """
    matched: int = 0
    record_only: list[str] = dataclasses.field(default_factory=list)
    measurement_only: list[str] = dataclasses.field(default_factory=list)
    calls_total: int = 0
    calls_covered: int = 0


def predicted_saving(record: Record, results: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """Predicted matmul time under native winners against tuned winners.

    Joined on the *signature* digest. The dispatch digest also mixes in the
    build's manifest hash and source revision (standards 13.1), so it does not
    survive across the two artifacts -- joining on it returns an empty result
    that reads like "these files share nothing" rather than like a bug.
    """
    calls = {o["signature"]: int(o.get("calls", 0)) for o in record.observations}
    native_name = {o["signature"]: o.get("native", "") for o in record.observations}

    coverage = Coverage(calls_total=sum(calls.values()))
    coverage.record_only = set(calls)

    rows, by_family = [], collections.defaultdict(
        lambda: {"calls": 0, "native_us": 0.0, "tuned_us": 0.0}
    )
    native_total = tuned_total = 0.0

    for result in results:
        signature = result.get("signature")
        if not signature:
            continue                       # pre-HI23 file: nothing to join on
        if signature not in calls:
            coverage.measurement_only.append(signature)
            continue
        coverage.record_only.discard(signature)

        ok = {
            c["name"]: c
            for c in result.get("candidates", [])
            if c.get("status") == "ok" and c.get("median_us")
        }
        native = ok.get(result.get("native") or native_name.get(signature, ""))
        winner = ok.get(result.get("winner", ""))
        if native is None or winner is None:
            # A rejected signature has no baseline, so it supports no claim.
            # Counted as uncovered rather than as a zero saving: those are
            # different statements and only one of them is true.
            continue

        n = int(calls[signature])
        coverage.matched += 1
        coverage.calls_covered += n
        native_us = n * float(native["median_us"])
        tuned_us = n * float(winner["median_us"])
        native_total += native_us
        tuned_total += tuned_us

        family = (result.get("winner", "") or "").split(":", 1)[0]
        bucket = by_family[family]
        bucket["calls"] += n
        bucket["native_us"] += native_us
        bucket["tuned_us"] += tuned_us

        rows.append(
            {
                "signature": signature,
                "calls": n,
                "saved_us_each": float(native["median_us"]) - float(winner["median_us"]),
                "saved_us": native_us - tuned_us,
                "winner": result.get("winner", ""),
                "margin_pct": result.get("improvement_pct", 0.0),
            }
        )

    rows.sort(key=lambda r: (-r["saved_us"], r["signature"]))
    slower = [r for r in rows if r["saved_us"] < 0]
    return {
        "coverage": coverage,
        "native_total_us": native_total,
        "tuned_total_us": tuned_total,
        "saving_pct": (
            100.0 * (native_total - tuned_total) / native_total
            if native_total > 0 else 0.0
        ),
        "by_family": dict(by_family),
        "rows": rows,
        "slower": slower,
    }
```

`slower` is not a diagnostic curiosity. If any signature predicts a slower
total, either a winner was selected against a stale baseline or the two
artifacts are mismatched — fail the command loudly rather than netting it off
against the wins and reporting a smaller positive number.

#### The interval, once HI12's E2 samples are in the file

```python
def saving_interval(record, results, draws=2000, seed=0):
    """Bootstrap the predicted total over the raw per-round samples.

    A median times an exact call count is not an exact product: the median has
    a sampling distribution and 121 of them are being summed. Reporting '10.2%'
    without a band asserts more than 15 samples per signature support.

    Returns None when the file has no `samples_us` -- every artifact predating
    HI12's E2, including the ones RV19 was computed from. An interval invented
    from MAD instead would be narrower than the truth and would look like the
    real thing, which is worse than not having one.
    """
    usable = [
        r for r in results
        if any("samples_us" in c for c in r.get("candidates", []))
    ]
    if not usable:
        return None

    rng = random.Random(seed)
    calls = {o["signature"]: int(o.get("calls", 0)) for o in record.observations}
    totals = []
    for _ in range(draws):
        native_total = tuned_total = 0.0
        for result in usable:
            pair = _native_and_winner_samples(result)
            if pair is None:
                continue
            native_samples, winner_samples = pair
            n = calls.get(result["signature"], 0)
            native_total += n * statistics.median(
                rng.choices(native_samples, k=len(native_samples))
            )
            tuned_total += n * statistics.median(
                rng.choices(winner_samples, k=len(winner_samples))
            )
        if native_total > 0:
            totals.append(100.0 * (native_total - tuned_total) / native_total)
    totals.sort()
    return totals[int(0.025 * len(totals))], totals[int(0.975 * len(totals))]


def _finite(samples: list[float | None]) -> list[float]:
    """Strip the nulls.

    `samples_us` is round-aligned and NaN-padded on the C++ side, serialised as
    JSON `null` for a round where the candidate failed to launch
    (hip-autotune-tuner.cu:377-391). The padding exists so HI12's paired sign
    test compares round r against round r; a bootstrap over unpaired medians
    does not need the alignment and must not resample the nulls.
    """
    return [s for s in samples if s is not None]
```

# rocprofv3's CSV column names have moved between point releases (Kernel_Name
# vs "Kernel Name", Start_Timestamp vs BeginNs). Key by header, never by
# position, and say which columns were found -- a silently mis-parsed trace
# produces a plausible percentage.
_NAME_COLUMNS = ("Kernel_Name", "Kernel Name", "KernelName")
_START_COLUMNS = ("Start_Timestamp", "BeginNs", "Start")
_END_COLUMNS = ("End_Timestamp", "EndNs", "End")
_AGENT_COLUMNS = ("Agent_Id", "Device_Id", "gpu-id")


# Family attribution. The quantize kernels are the part that is easy to get
# wrong and that decides whether this agrees with RV19 at all: standards 7.1
# times the *complete path*, and MMQ's activation quantisation is a large
# fraction of its cost. Attributing quantize_mmq_q8_1 to "other" would
# understate the matmul fraction and put this method into disagreement with a
# measurement that did include it.
_FAMILY_PATTERNS = (
    ("mmq", ("mul_mat_q", "quantize_mmq_q8_1")),
    ("mmvq", ("mul_mat_vec_q", "quantize_q8_1")),
    ("mmvf", ("mul_mat_vec_f",)),
    ("mmf", ("mul_mat_f",)),
    ("blas", ("Cijk_", "rocblas", "hipblas", "gemm")),
    ("attention", ("flash_attn", "soft_max")),
    ("norm/rope/act", ("rms_norm", "norm_f32", "rope", "silu", "gelu", "swiglu")),
    ("copy/other", ("cpy_", "dup_", "contiguous", "get_rows", "add_f32", "mul_f32")),
)


def classify(kernel_name: str) -> str:
    for family, needles in _FAMILY_PATTERNS:
        if any(needle in kernel_name for needle in needles):
            return family
    return "unmapped"

```python
def gpu_busy_ns(spans: list[tuple[int, int]]) -> int:
    """Union of kernel intervals, not their sum.

    Kernels from different queues overlap, so summing durations double-counts
    and can exceed wall time -- which then produces a GPU utilisation above
    100% and a wall-clock ceiling that is nonsense. Merge first.
    """
    if not spans:
        return 0
    spans = sorted(spans)
    busy = 0
    start, end = spans[0]
    for lo, hi in spans[1:]:
        if lo > end:
            busy += end - start
            start, end = lo, hi
        else:
            end = max(end, hi)
    return busy + (end - start)
```

### Part 3 — the power calculation, as a script rather than a claim

```python
def repetitions_needed(effect_pct, spread_pct, power=0.8, alpha=0.05, paired_r=0.0):
    """Repetitions per arm to resolve `effect_pct` against `spread_pct`.

    Run before an A/B, not after one. HANDOFF's '50+ repetitions' predates any
    estimate of the effect size; at a 3% effect against the observed 10.2%
    spread the real figure is ~185 per arm, and at 1% it is over 1600.

    `paired_r` is the correlation between arms when rounds are interleaved
    within one session, which is the one lever that helps: pairing removes the
    shared machine-state variance, and the required n falls by (1 - r). The
    2026-08-05 native/replay comparison was run this way; any A/B that does get
    run must be, and its analysis must use the pairing rather than comparing
    two means of independent runs.
    """
    z_a, z_b = 1.959964, 0.841621          # alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 0.8
    n = 2 * ((z_a + z_b) * spread_pct / effect_pct) ** 2
    return math.ceil(n * (1.0 - paired_r))
```

Against the figures already in HANDOFF — tg128 at 67.99 ± 6.92 t/s, so
s = 10.2% — and a matmul fraction of 30% against a 10% matmul saving, d = 3%:

```
unpaired          n = 2 x ((1.96 + 0.84) x 10.2 / 3.0)^2  =  181 per arm
paired at r = 0.7                                          =   55 per arm
at d = 1%, unpaired                                        = 1631 per arm
```

That table is the deliverable. It converts an open question into a cost, and a
cost can be declined explicitly — which is the outcome HANDOFF should record if
the measured fraction puts the effect near 1%.

## Validation

### Part 1 — the model

- **Reproduces RV19.** Run against the artifacts RV19 used and get 10.2%
  call-weighted with MMQ at ~96% of matmul time. A different answer means either
  the tool or the original hand calculation is wrong, and both are worth knowing.
- **Coverage is reported and correct.** Construct a case with a signature in the
  record and not the measurements, and the reverse; both appear in the counts and
  neither is silently dropped.
- **The Gemma E4B case is caught.** Running the model on the cache that produced
  21 hits and 100 misses must report low coverage prominently, not a healthy
  saving over the 21.
- **Ranking by saved time differs from ranking by margin** on the RV19 data.
  If the two orderings agree, the join is probably wrong — a +69.76% winner on
  13,440 calls and a +44.35% winner on 110,160 cannot rank the same way on both.
- **Never predicts a slower total.** If tuned exceeds native anywhere, either a
  winner was selected against a stale baseline or the artifacts are mismatched.
  Fail loudly rather than reporting a negative saving.
- Bootstrap interval brackets the point estimate and narrows as `final_samples`
  rises — compare a 3-sample tune against a 15-sample one on the same workload.

### Part 2 — the fraction

- Family shares sum to 100% of traced kernel time, with an explicit "unmapped"
  bucket. An unmapped share above a few percent means the symbol map is
  incomplete and the matmul fraction is understated.
- Prefill and decode are reported separately and differ. If they do not, the
  phase separation is not working — the recorded end-to-end results already show
  prefill and generation behaving differently.
- The mmq share is consistent with RV19's "MMQ is 96% of matmul time" from a
  completely independent measurement path. Two methods agreeing is the check
  that matters here.
- GPU utilisation is captured alongside, so the wall-clock ceiling can be stated
  and not just the kernel-time share.

### Part 3 — the decision

- The required repetition count is computed and written into HANDOFF.
- Either an A/B is run at that count with paired interleaved rounds, or HANDOFF
  states explicitly that the effect is below this benchmark's resolution and no
  further end-to-end numbers are quoted for it. Leaving the question open is the
  one unacceptable outcome, since that is the current state.

## Effort & Risk

M, and the lowest-risk item in the set: part 1 touches no GPU code, part 2
touches no code at all, part 3 is a function.

**It should probably go first.** Every other tuning item is measured by its
effect, and there is currently no instrument capable of measuring the effects
being produced. Building the instrument before the next round of improvements
costs a few days and makes every subsequent item assessable; building it after
means re-running everything to find out what any of it was worth.

**The risk worth naming:** the fraction may come back small. If matmul is 25% of
decode wall time, then even a further 10% matmul saving is 2.5% end to end and
the honest conclusion is that HI12/HI24/HI34 buy confidence and hygiene rather
than throughput, and that HI26 (removing work) is where remaining throughput
lives. That is a genuinely useful answer and the project is currently structured
not to receive it.

Depends on HI12 E2 for intervals, but the point estimate works today against
artifacts already on disk.
