# Adaptive screening with an impact budget and a same-kernel noise canary

## Description

Screening currently runs every candidate at `GGML_HIP_TUNE_SCREEN_SAMPLES` (3 by default), retains "native always + top 3 by median + everything within 10% of best", and measures only those at the final sample count. RV21 showed the measured error of a three-sample median is around **14%**, so the retention band is narrower than the error of the measurement that selects it. A candidate can be discarded on noise and never reconsidered.

Raising screening to 15 for everything fixes the incoherence and, measured, buys **nothing**: call-weighted saving moved 10.2% to 10.4% while MMVQ moved 0.6 points the other way, less than run-to-run variation. So uniform full sampling costs tuning time for no demonstrated benefit.

The proposal is to spend measurement effort where it can change the answer, and to detect when the measurement itself is untrustworthy.

## Steps

1. Expose `mul_mat_q_compute_J_best` (or a wrapper returning it) so the tuner can identify which forced MMQ candidate corresponds to native's choice.
2. Implement the canary: after full measurement, compare native against the forced-`J_best` candidate when both were measured; if divergence exceeds the threshold, re-measure both and record the fact.
3. Record canary divergence per signature in the measurements JSONL so it can be audited offline, and surface a summary count at end of run.
4. Implement the double-native repeatability check for families without a known same-kernel pair.
5. Add a hot-list input (`GGML_HIP_TUNE_HOT_SIGNATURES`), a signature-digest to call-count map emitted by the inventory step.
6. Compute `impact = call_count × native_median_us`; for signatures above a configurable share of total impact, measure all eligible candidates at full samples.
7. Replace the fixed 10% retention band with one derived from observed screen spread.
8. Default the thresholds conservatively and make them env-tunable, consistent with the existing sample-count knobs.

## Detailed Solution & Technical Design

### Adaptive measurement

1. Screen all eligible candidates at 3 samples.
2. Fully measure the provisional winner **and** native.
3. **Retest any candidate close enough that screening noise could change the outcome.** With ~14% screen error, "close enough" is a band derived from the observed screen spread rather than a fixed 10% — if a candidate's screening median is within the error bar of the provisional winner's, it has not actually been ruled out.
4. Use broader full measurement only for high-impact signatures.

### Impact budget

Rank by estimated time contribution, not call count:

```
impact = call_count × native_median_us
```

Call count alone is misleading. From the recorded workload: `5120x24 . 5120x512` runs 13,440 times at 38.8 us against `5120x8704 . 5120x512` at 11,960 times and 685 us — similar call counts, an order of magnitude apart in time. Ranking by calls would spend the budget on the wrong one.

For the top signatures by impact, measure **every** eligible candidate at full samples; that is cheap insurance where it matters. The tail keeps aggressive screening.

`call_count` is not available in-process at tuning time — the tuner tunes a signature on first encounter and cannot know its future frequency. It *is* available from record mode, which already emits `calls` per signature. So the tuner needs the hot list as an input, keyed by signature digest, produced by the existing record -> inventory step. That fits the pipeline as it stands and needs no new instrumentation.

### Same-kernel noise canary

The strongest part of this. `mul_mat_q_switch_J` computes `J_best`, overwrites it with `forced_J` when non-zero, and calls one launcher — so **native and the forced candidate at `J_best` are the same kernel**. Any difference between their medians is measurement error, not a real effect. That gives a self-calibrating check needing no external reference:

```
noise = |native_median - forced_Jbest_median| / native_median
```

If that exceeds roughly 3-5% after full measurement, the signature's measurements are untrustworthy: re-measure both. This catches thermal drift, sequencing effects, insufficient synchronisation and inadequate sample counts, without assuming every candidate needs full sampling.

RV21 is the worked example: native 465.80 us against `j128` 531.40 us, a 14% divergence between identical kernels, which the canary would have flagged immediately. At 15 samples they converged to 462.28 and 464.92, a 0.6% spread — comfortably inside the threshold.

**A more general variant worth considering alongside it:** measure native twice as two independent entries and compare. That is the same kernel by construction, needs no knowledge of `J_best`, and works for every family rather than MMQ alone. It measures pure repeatability. The `J_best` form is strictly stronger where it applies, because it also verifies the forced path adds no overhead — an invariant worth holding, since the whole dispatch design rests on it. Both are cheap; the two together cost one extra full measurement per signature.

## Validation

- Canary fires on a deliberately noisy run (tune while another process loads the GPU) and does not fire on a quiet one.
- On the RV21 signature at 3 screening samples the canary flags divergence; at 15 it does not.
- Adaptive screening reproduces the 15-sample result (10.4% call-weighted) at materially lower tuning time than uniform 15-sample screening.
- Hot-list ranking by `call_count × native_median_us` puts `5120x512 . 5120x512` (110,160 calls, 26.4%) at the top and does not promote high-count cheap signatures above low-count expensive ones.
- Measurements JSONL carries canary divergence per signature.

## Effort & Risk

M. The canary is self-contained, needs no new inputs, and is the highest value per unit of work — do it first. The impact budget needs the record-to-tune hot-list plumbing, which is new but small and fits the existing pipeline. The retention-band change is the riskiest, since it alters which candidates reach final measurement; gate it behind the canary so a bad band shows up as flagged noise rather than silently worse winners.

Measured context for expectations: uniform 15-sample screening changed the aggregate by 0.2 points, within run-to-run variation. **This work should be justified as buying confidence in per-signature results and protection against silent mis-measurement, not as a performance gain.**

## Standards

7.4 (hot-signature ranking) is the basis for the impact budget, and this sharpens it: rank by time contribution rather than frequency. 12.4 (hard eligibility before launch) is unaffected. The screening retention policy currently documented in HI12 ("native always + top 3 by median + everything within 10% of best") is what this supersedes.

## Notes

Proposed by the owner after RV21 established that a three-sample median carries roughly 14% error while the retention band was 10%.

The canary rests on an invariant proven in RV21: the patched `mul_mat_q_switch_J` runs identical code for native and for a forced J, so forcing `J = J_best` is native rather than merely equivalent to it. If that invariant is ever broken by an upstream change to the switch, the canary will fire constantly — which is itself the correct alarm.
