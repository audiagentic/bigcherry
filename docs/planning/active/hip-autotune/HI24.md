# Adaptive screening with an impact budget and a same-kernel noise canary

## Description

Screening currently runs every candidate at `GGML_HIP_TUNE_SCREEN_SAMPLES` (3 by default), retains "native always + top 3 by median + everything within 10% of best", and measures only those at the final sample count. RV21 showed the measured error of a three-sample median is around **14%**, so the retention band is narrower than the error of the measurement that selects it. A candidate can be discarded on noise and never reconsidered.

Raising screening to 15 for everything fixes the incoherence and, measured, buys **nothing**: call-weighted saving moved 10.2% to 10.4% while MMVQ moved 0.6 points the other way, less than run-to-run variation. So uniform full sampling costs tuning time for no demonstrated benefit.

The proposal is to spend measurement effort where it can change the answer, and to detect when the measurement itself is untrustworthy.

## Detailed Solution & Technical Design

### Adaptive measurement

1. Screen all eligible candidates at 3 samples.
2. Fully measure the provisional winner **and** native.
3. **Retest any candidate close enough that screening noise could change the
   outcome.** With ~14% screen error, "close enough" is a band derived from
   observed noise rather than a fixed 10% — if a candidate's screening median is
   within the error bar of the provisional winner's, it has not been ruled out.
4. Use broader full measurement only for high-impact signatures.

---

### The band, derived rather than guessed (step 7)

The canary already measures exactly the quantity the band needs. It is the
divergence between two measurements of *the same kernel*, so it is the harness's
own error, expressed as a percentage, for this signature, on this machine, at
this sample count. That is the definition of "how far apart can two candidates
read before the difference is meaningless".

```
band_pct = clamp(2 x canary_pct, screen_keep_within_pct, band_max_pct)
```

defaults `screen_keep_within_pct = 10` (floor, unchanged) and
`band_max_pct = 50` (ceiling, so a pathological run retains everything rather
than nothing).

Against the two known data points: RV21's contaminated 3-sample run had
`canary_pct = 14`, giving a 28% band that would have retained the candidates the
fixed 10% band discarded on noise. A quiet 15-sample run had `canary_pct = 0.6`,
which floors at 10% and reproduces today's behaviour exactly. The band widens
precisely when the measurement is untrustworthy and not otherwise, which is the
property a fixed constant cannot have.

#### One ordering problem to solve first

The canary currently runs *after* the final stage (`hip-autotune-tuner.cu:506`),
and the band is needed *before* it, at retention. Both members of the canary pair
are screened anyway, so compute a **screen-stage canary** from the screening
medians, use it for the band, and keep the existing final-stage canary for the
trustworthiness report. Record both: `canary_screen_pct` and `canary_pct`.
Divergence between the two is itself informative — it says the extra samples
changed the answer, which is the case for raising `screen_samples`.

When no canary pair exists (step 4 not yet landed, or a non-MMQ native),
fall back to the dispersion already computed during screening:

```
spread = median over screened candidates of (mad_us / median_us)
band_pct = clamp(2 x 100 x spread, screen_keep_within_pct, band_max_pct)
```

### Impact budget

Rank by estimated time contribution, not call count:

```
impact = call_count x native_median_us
```

Call count alone is misleading. From the recorded workload: `5120x24 . 5120x512`
runs 13,440 times at 38.8 us against `5120x8704 . 5120x512` at 11,960 times and
685 us — similar call counts, an order of magnitude apart in time. Ranking by
calls would spend the budget on the wrong one.

#### The bootstrap problem, and the field that already solves it

`native_median_us` comes from a tuning run, and the first tuning run does not
have one. So the ranking runs in two passes:

**Pass 1 — no prior measurements.** Rank by `calls x est_bytes`.
`est_bytes` is already recorded per observation
(`hip-autotune-record.cpp:190-196`) and already used as the secondary sort key in
the record report (`:226`). It is the memory traffic the operation implies, and
every shape in the hot list is bandwidth-bound at these sizes, so it is a good
proxy — crucially, it discriminates exactly the case HI24 was written about:
`5120x24` and `5120x8704` differ by 360x in `est_bytes` and barely at all in
calls.

**Pass 2 — a previous measurements file exists.** Rank by
`calls x native_median_us`, joining the record JSONL to the measurements JSONL on
signature digest. Strictly better, and available from the second tune onward.

No new instrumentation either way. Pass 1 uses a field recorded since HI10;
pass 2 uses a file the tuner already writes.

#### Tiers

Sort descending by impact, accumulate share, and assign:

| Tier | Cumulative impact share | Policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | up to `hot_share_pct` (default 80) | **skip screening**; measure every eligible candidate at `final_samples` |
| B | next, down to `cold_share_pct` (default 0.1) | screen, adaptive band, `final_samples` on finalists |
| C | remainder, and anything absent from the hot list | screen, retain native + top 1, `max(final_samples / 4, 5)` |

Tier A skipping screening is the point. On the RV19 workload the top three
signatures are 26.4%, 14.8% and 13.2% of all calls — **54% of the workload in
three signatures**, each with 11 eligible candidates. Measuring all 11 at full
samples costs seconds and removes screening noise from the decisions that matter
most. The 1000-signature tail keeps aggressive screening and pays for it.

`TUNING-DETAIL.md` shows why the tail deserves less: below the top 15,
signatures run under 1.3% of calls each, and the whole distribution has
416,940 calls across 121 signatures.

#### Absent from the hot list is not an error

`test-backend-ops` sweeps produce 1151 signatures against a hot list of ~121
from a real workload. Unlisted signatures fall to tier C rather than being
skipped — the synthetic sweep is still the correctness vehicle and must keep
running to completion. Log the counts (`hot list matched N of M signatures`);
a very low match rate means the hot list came from a different workload, which
is exactly the mistake HANDOFF records for the Gemma E4B cache (21 hits, 100
misses).

### Same-kernel noise canary

The strongest part of this. `mul_mat_q_switch_J` computes `J_best`, overwrites
it with `forced_J` when non-zero, and calls one launcher — so **native and the
forced candidate at `J_best` are the same kernel**. Any difference between their
medians is measurement error, not a real effect. That gives a self-calibrating
check needing no external reference:

```
noise = |native_median - forced_Jbest_median| / native_median
```

If that exceeds roughly 3-5% after full measurement, the signature's
measurements are untrustworthy: re-measure both. This catches thermal drift,
sequencing effects, insufficient synchronisation and inadequate sample counts,
without assuming every candidate needs full sampling.

RV21 is the worked example: native 465.80 us against `j128` 531.40 us, a 14%
divergence between identical kernels, which the canary would have flagged
immediately. At 15 samples they converged to 462.28 and 464.92, a 0.6% spread —
comfortably inside the threshold.

#### Step 4 matters more than it looks

The `J_best` canary only applies where native is MMQ. On the RV19 workload that
is 11 of 121 signatures by count; every MMVQ, MMVF and BLAS signature records
`canary_pct = -1`. So the calibration is currently absent from the families
where the *margins are tightest* — `TUNING-DETAIL.md`'s MMVQ tables rank
neighbours 0.3%, 0.8% and 1.1% apart, decisions far finer than the MMQ ones the
canary does cover.

The double-native form fixes that and is trivial: enter native twice as two
finalist entries under distinct labels, let the existing interleaved final stage
measure both, and compare. It is the same kernel by construction for every
family, needs no knowledge of `J_best`, and costs one extra finalist slot. The
`J_best` form stays where it applies because it is strictly stronger — it also
verifies the forced path adds no overhead, the invariant the whole dispatch
design rests on.

### Prerequisite shared by HI24, HI35 and HI36 — emit `native` in the result line

measurements JSONL does not say which candidate was native.** A result line

### Hot list format — flat text, not JSONL

The item originally specified JSON Lines "matching every other artifact". That
is right for artifacts the *tools* read and wrong here, because this is the only
artifact the **C++ side reads**, and:

> There is no JSON parser anywhere in the overlay. `hip-autotune-record.cpp`,
> `hip-autotune-signature.cpp` and `hip-autotune-tuner.cu` all *write* JSON by
> hand; `hip-autotune-replay.cpp` reads a binary format with explicit byte
> offsets. Nothing reads JSON.

Adding a hand-rolled JSON scanner to the tuner to read four fixed fields buys
nothing and is a new class of parsing bug in a file that decides how much
measurement each signature gets. Use a flat format that `sscanf` handles in one
line, with the metadata in comment lines:

```text
# bigcherry hot list, schema 1
# basis calls_x_native_median
# source_record artifacts/mtp-server-inventory.json
# source_measurements /tmp/t.jsonl.measurements.jsonl
# signatures 121
# columns signature share_pct cum_share_pct rank
035282e415599068a1b2c3d4e5f60718 35.4300 35.4300 1
4dc5d20446ffea2f0011223344556677 29.8200 65.2500 2
```

`basis` is `calls_x_est_bytes` on pass 1 and `calls_x_native_median` on pass 2,
and the tuner logs which it loaded — a pass-1 list is a weaker ranking and a run
must not silently pretend otherwise.

Note what the two rows show: ranked by **calls**, `4dc5d204…` leads. Ranked by
**impact**, `035282e4…` does, despite 44% fewer calls. That reordering is the
entire point of the item.

Signatures are the full 32-hex-character signature digest, **not** the dispatch
digest: the dispatch digest mixes in this build's manifest hash and source
revision (standards 13.1), so it cannot be joined across the two artifacts. The
tuner has the signature digest at `hip-autotune-tuner.cu:451`, computed for
exactly this class of cross-artifact join.

### Emitting it — `tools/bigcherry/inventory.py`

```python
def _native_medians(measurements: Path, record: Record) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Native's median per *signature* digest, from a measurements JSONL.

    Native is identified in preference order: the result line's own `native`
    field; failing that, the record's `observation.native` for the same
    signature. Never by scanning for `*:native:v1` -- a cross-family result
    carries one per family and the wrong pick silently re-bases every impact
    figure in the file.
    """
    from . import report

    by_signature = {
        o["signature"]: o.get("native", "") for o in record.observations
    }
    medians: dict[str, float] = {}
    for result in report.read_measurements_jsonl(measurements):
        signature = result.get("signature")
        if not signature:
            continue                       # pre-HI23 file: no signature digest
        native_name = result.get("native") or by_signature.get(signature)
        if not native_name:
            continue
        for candidate in result.get("candidates", []):
            if candidate.get("name") != native_name:
                continue
            median = candidate.get("median_us")
            if median:
                medians[signature] = float(median)
            break
    return medians


def write_hot_list(
    record: Record,
    output: Path,
    *,
    measurements: Path | None = None,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """Rank observed signatures by estimated time contribution.

    Two bases, because the first tuning run has no timings to weight with:

      pass 1  calls x est_bytes         -- est_bytes is recorded per observation
                                           since HI10 (hip-autotune-record.cpp:120)
                                           and is a bandwidth proxy. It separates
                                           5120x24 from 5120x8704 by 360x, which
                                           is precisely what call count cannot.
      pass 2  calls x native_median_us  -- available from the second tune on.

    Ranking by calls alone puts a 13,440-call 38.8 us signature above an
    11,960-call 685 us one. That inversion is why this function exists.
    """
    native_median = (
        _native_medians(measurements, record)
        if measurements is not None and measurements.is_file()
        else {}
    )
    basis = "calls_x_native_median" if native_median else "calls_x_est_bytes"

    rows: list[dict[str, Any]] = []
    for observation in record.observations:
        signature = observation["signature"]
        calls = float(observation.get("calls", 0))
        weight = native_median.get(signature)
        if weight is None:
            weight = float(observation.get("est_bytes", 0))
        rows.append(
            {
                "signature": signature,
                "calls": int(calls),
                "native_median_us": native_median.get(signature),
                "impact": calls * weight,
            }
        )

    rows.sort(key=lambda r: (-r["impact"], r["signature"]))
    total = sum(r["impact"] for r in rows) or 1.0
    cumulative = 0.0
    for rank, row in enumerate(rows, start=1):
        row["share_pct"] = 100.0 * row["impact"] / total
        cumulative += row["share_pct"]
        row["cum_share_pct"] = cumulative
        row["rank"] = rank

    lines = [
        "# bigcherry hot list, schema 1",
        f"# basis {basis}",
        f"# source_measurements {measurements if measurements else '-'}",
        f"# signatures {len(rows)}",
        "# columns signature share_pct cum_share_pct rank",
    ]
    lines += [
        f"{r['signature']} {r['share_pct']:.4f} "
        f"{r['cum_share_pct']:.4f} {r['rank']}"
        for r in rows
    ]
    output.write_text("\n".join(lines) + "\n", encoding="utf-8", newline="\n")
    return {"basis": basis, "signatures": len(rows), "rows": rows}
```

The sort key is `(-impact, signature)`, not `-impact` alone: a tie broken by
dict insertion order would make the file non-reproducible across runs, and this
file is an input to tuning decisions.

### Loading it — `hip-autotune-tuner.cu`

In the anonymous namespace, after `DigestEqual` (`:118-122`), which it reuses:

```cpp
struct HotEntry {
    double share_pct;
    double cum_share_pct;
    int    rank;
};

struct HotList {
    std::unordered_map<ggml_hip_digest, HotEntry, DigestHash, DigestEqual> entries;
    std::string basis;
};

// Match rate is reported at the end of the run, so it has to survive across
// signatures. Atomics rather than plain counters because two device contexts
// tune concurrently and these are outside g_mutex.
std::atomic<size_t> g_hot_probed{0};
std::atomic<size_t> g_hot_matched{0};

bool digest_from_hex(const char * hex, ggml_hip_digest & out) {
    if (strlen(hex) != 2 * GGML_HIP_DIGEST_BYTES) return false;
    for (size_t i = 0; i < GGML_HIP_DIGEST_BYTES; ++i) {
        unsigned int byte = 0;
        if (sscanf(hex + 2 * i, "%2x", &byte) != 1) return false;
        out.bytes[i] = (uint8_t) byte;
    }
    return true;
}

const HotList & hot_list() {
    static const HotList list = [] {
        HotList l;
        const char * path = getenv("GGML_HIP_TUNE_HOT_SIGNATURES");
        if (path == nullptr || path[0] == '\0') {
            return l;   // no list: every signature is tier B, today's behaviour
        }
        FILE * file = fopen(path, "r");
        if (file == nullptr) {
            GGML_LOG_WARN("bigcherry: cannot read hot list '%s'; every "
                          "signature will be tuned at tier B\n", path);
            return l;
        }
        char line[256];
        while (fgets(line, sizeof(line), file) != nullptr) {
            if (line[0] == '#') {
                char basis[64] = {0};
                if (sscanf(line, "# basis %63s", basis) == 1) l.basis = basis;
                continue;
            }
            char hex[64] = {0};
            HotEntry entry = {};
            if (sscanf(line, "%63s %lf %lf %d", hex, &entry.share_pct,
                       &entry.cum_share_pct, &entry.rank) != 4) {
                continue;
            }
            ggml_hip_digest digest;
            if (digest_from_hex(hex, digest)) l.entries.emplace(digest, entry);
        }
        fclose(file);
        GGML_LOG_INFO("bigcherry: hot list '%s' -- %zu signature(s), basis %s\n",
                      path, l.entries.size(),
                      l.basis.empty() ? "unstated" : l.basis.c_str());
        return l;
    }();
    return list;
}

enum HotTier { TIER_A, TIER_B, TIER_C };

// Absent from a *loaded* list means tier C: test-backend-ops produces 1151
// signatures against a hot list of ~121 from a real workload, and the synthetic
// sweep is still the correctness vehicle, so it must run to completion rather
// than be skipped.
//
// Absent *list* means tier B for everything, which is byte-identical to
// today's behaviour. Collapsing the two cases would make an unset environment
// variable silently downgrade every signature in the project.
HotTier tier_for(const ggml_hip_digest & signature_digest,
                 const ggml_hip_tuner_config & config) {
    const HotList & list = hot_list();
    if (list.entries.empty()) return TIER_B;
    g_hot_probed.fetch_add(1, std::memory_order_relaxed);
    const auto found = list.entries.find(signature_digest);
    if (found == list.entries.end())                           return TIER_C;
    g_hot_matched.fetch_add(1, std::memory_order_relaxed);
    if (found->second.cum_share_pct <= config.hot_share_pct)    return TIER_A;
    if (found->second.share_pct     <  config.cold_share_pct)   return TIER_C;
    return TIER_B;
}
```

Needs `#include <atomic>`; `<cstring>`, `<cstdio>` and `<cstdlib>` are already
there.

```cpp
        const double band_pct = retention_band_pct(result, survivors, config);
        const double best = survivors.empty() ? 0.0 : survivors.front()->median_us;
        for (size_t i = 0; i < survivors.size(); ++i) {
            Measurement * m = survivors[i];
            const bool is_native = m == native_m;
            const bool in_top    = (int) i < config.screen_keep_top;
            const bool near_best = best > 0.0 &&
                m->median_us <= best * (1.0 + band_pct / 100.0);
            if (is_native || in_top || near_best) finalists.push_back(m);
        }
    }
```

Note `m == native_m` in place of `m->candidate == native.candidate`. Step 4
introduces a second `Measurement` sharing native's descriptor pointer, at which
point the old test matches two rows; every such test must move to the
`Measurement *` identity. The sites are `:604`, `:660` and `:852-853`.

### Step 4 — the double-native twin

Extract the twin search first, so the screen-stage and final-stage canaries
share one definition rather than two copies that can drift:

```cpp
// Native's same-kernel twin: `mul_mat_q_switch_J` overwrites J_best with
// forced_J and calls one launcher, so forcing J == J_best *is* native rather
// than merely equivalent to it (RV21). Any gap between their medians is
// measurement error by construction.
Measurement * find_canary_twin(const std::vector<Measurement *> & pool,
                               const Measurement * native_m,
                               const ggml_hip_native_selection & native,
                               const ggml_hip_dispatch_signature_v1 & sig) {
    if (native.candidate == nullptr ||
            native.candidate->family != GGML_HIP_FAMILY_MMQ) {
        return nullptr;
    }
    const bool fb = (sig.ne0[1] % 128) != 0;
    const int j_best = ggml_cuda_mmq_native_j_best(
        (ggml_type) sig.src0_type, fb, sig.ned[1]);
    if (j_best == 0) return nullptr;
    for (Measurement * m : pool) {
        if (m == native_m || !m->measured || m->candidate == nullptr) continue;
        if (m->candidate->family == GGML_HIP_FAMILY_MMQ &&
                m->candidate->variant.primary == j_best &&
                (m->candidate->variant.fallback != 0) == fb) {
            return m;
        }
    }
    return nullptr;
}
```

The double-native form covers the families the `J_best` form cannot. It is a
second `Measurement` over the *same descriptor*, which imposes three
constraints worth stating before writing it:

1. **It must be appended before `screening` is built.** `screening` holds
   `Measurement *` into `result.measurements`; a later `push_back` reallocates
   the vector and invalidates every one of them.
2. **`native_m` must be found by first match, and twins skipped.** Otherwise
   the twin can become the baseline.
3. **It must be excluded from winner selection.** A twin winning would record
   `mmq:native:v1` as a measured winner over itself.

```cpp
// Measurement, new field:
```

### Step 7 — the band

```cpp
// The canary is the harness's own error for this signature, at this sample
// count, on this machine -- which is the definition of "how far apart can two
// candidates read before the difference is meaningless". A fixed constant
// cannot have that property; RV21's run had a 14% error against a 10% band.
double retention_band_pct(const Result & r,
                          const std::vector<Measurement *> & screened,
                          const ggml_hip_tuner_config & config) {
    double basis = 0.0;
    if (r.canary_screen_pct >= 0.0) {
        basis = 2.0 * r.canary_screen_pct;
    } else {
        // No same-kernel pair reached screening. Fall back to the dispersion
        // screening already produced -- weaker, because a screen-stage MAD
        // over `screen_samples` points is itself a noisy statistic, which is
        // why step 4 is sequenced before this one.
        std::vector<double> ratios;
        for (const Measurement * m : screened) {
            if (m->measured && m->median_us > 0.0) {
                ratios.push_back(m->mad_us / m->median_us);
            }
        }
        basis = 2.0 * 100.0 * median_of(ratios);
    }
    // Clamped below at the existing constant so a zero basis -- no canary and
    // no usable dispersion -- retains what today retains rather than retaining
    // native alone, which would silently disable tuning for that signature.
    return std::clamp(basis, config.screen_keep_within_pct, config.band_max_pct);
}
```

The screen-stage canary that feeds it runs immediately after the screening loop
and before retention, using the same `find_canary_twin` over `survivors`:

```cpp
    {
        Measurement * twin = find_canary_twin(survivors, native_m, native, sig);
        if (twin == nullptr && config.double_native) {
            for (Measurement * m : survivors) {
                if (m->is_native_twin) { twin = m; break; }
            }
        }
        if (twin != nullptr && native_m->median_us > 0.0) {
            result.canary_screen_pct = 100.0 *
                std::fabs(native_m->median_us - twin->median_us) /
                native_m->median_us;
            result.canary_screen_pair = twin->is_native_twin
                ? "native#twin" : twin->candidate->stable_name;
        }
    }
```

Divergence between `canary_screen_pct` and the final-stage `canary_pct` is
itself informative: it says the extra samples changed the answer, which is the
case for raising `screen_samples` at all.

### Step 8 — the end-of-run summary

The canary fields are per signature and nothing aggregates them, so nobody reads
them. Aggregate in `ggml_hip_tuner_flush`, which already walks `g_results` under
`g_mutex`:

```cpp
    // A per-signature field nobody aggregates is a field nobody reads. This is
    // the line that says whether the sweep's numbers can be believed at all.
    size_t checked = 0, flagged = 0, retried = 0;
    double worst = 0.0;
    std::string worst_signature;
    for (const auto & entry : g_results) {
        const Result & r = entry.second;
        if (r.canary_pct < 0.0) continue;
        ++checked;
        if (r.canary_pct > config.noise_canary_pct) ++flagged;
        if (r.canary_retries > 0) ++retried;
        if (r.canary_pct > worst) {
            worst = r.canary_pct;
            worst_signature = ggml_hip_digest_hex(r.signature_digest);
        }
    }
    GGML_LOG_INFO("bigcherry: canary -- %zu/%zu signatures checked, %zu flagged "
                  "above %.1f%%, worst %.1f%% (%s), %zu re-measured; "
                  "hot list matched %zu/%zu\n",
                  checked, g_results.size(), flagged, config.noise_canary_pct,
                  worst, worst_signature.c_str(), retried,
                  g_hot_matched.load(), g_hot_probed.load());
```

A low `checked/total` ratio is the signal that step 4 is still outstanding: with
only the `J_best` form, MMVQ, MMVF, MMF and BLAS signatures are never checked.
A low `matched/probed` ratio is the signal that the hot list came from a
different workload — the Gemma E4B mistake, 21 hits against 100 misses.

## Validation

### Canary (steps 2-4, 8)

- Canary fires on a deliberately noisy run (tune while another process loads the
  GPU) and does not fire on a quiet one.
- On the RV21 signature `6608d41c90aa4da1` at 3 screening samples the canary
  flags divergence; at 15 it does not.
- Measurements JSONL carries `canary_pct` and `canary_screen_pct` per signature.
- **After step 4**, `canary_pct >= 0` for every signature in a sweep, not only
  the MMQ ones. Today it is `-1` for MMVQ, MMVF, MMF and BLAS, which is the
  majority — measure the before/after ratio and record it.
- The double-native pair must agree with the `J_best` pair on MMQ signatures
  where both exist. Systematic disagreement means one of the two is measuring
  something other than repeatability.
- End-of-run summary line present, and its flagged count matches a Python
  aggregate over the JSONL.

### Hot list (steps 5-6)

- Pass-1 ranking (`calls x est_bytes`) and pass-2 ranking
  (`calls x native_median_us`) agree on the top 5 signatures of the RV19
  workload. If they disagree badly, `est_bytes` is a poor proxy and pass 1 needs
  rethinking before it is trusted for a first tune.
- Ranking must place `035282e415599068` above `4dc5d20446ffea2f` despite the
  latter having 78% more calls (61,800 x 271.28 us against 110,160 x 128.08 us).
  Getting this inversion right is the item's whole justification; a ranking that
  reproduces call order has silently fallen back to counting.
- `5120x24 . 5120x512` (13,440 calls, 38.8 us) must **not** outrank
  `5120x8704 . 5120x512` (11,960 calls, 685 us).
- Tier A covers the intended share: with `hot_share_pct = 80` the RV19 workload
  puts roughly the top 10 signatures in tier A, not 1 and not 100.
- A hot list from a *different* workload produces a low match rate and says so.
  Use the Gemma E4B case deliberately: the cache that gave 21 hits and 100
  misses should produce a visibly poor match line, not a silent tier-C sweep.
- With `GGML_HIP_TUNE_HOT_SIGNATURES` unset, behaviour is byte-identical to
  today's — every signature is tier B.

### Band (step 7)

- On a quiet 15-sample run the band floors at 10% and finalist sets are
  unchanged from today.
- On the RV21 3-sample run the band widens to ~28% and retains candidates the
  fixed band discarded.
- Band never exceeds `band_max_pct`, and a signature with no canary and no
  usable dispersion falls back to the floor rather than to zero. A zero band
  retains only native and would silently disable tuning for that signature.

### Aggregate

- Adaptive screening reproduces the 15-sample result (10.4% call-weighted) at
  materially lower tuning time than uniform 15-sample screening. Compare
  call-weighted saving, **not** win counts — HI12's E1 changes win counts for
  unrelated reasons and the two must not be conflated.
- Tier A signatures show `eligible == measured` (nothing screened out) while
  tier C signatures do not.

## Effort & Risk

M, and now clearly split by risk.

**Shipped and low risk:** the canary (steps 1-3). Self-contained, needs no new
inputs, highest value per unit of work — done first, as planned.

**Low risk, high value, do next:** step 4 (double-native). Twenty lines, and it
extends calibration from ~9% of signatures to all of them. The families it adds
are the ones deciding the finest margins — `TUNING-DETAIL.md`'s MMVQ tables rank
neighbours 0.3% apart while the covered MMQ ones decide at 2-3%.

**Moderate:** the impact budget (steps 5-6). New plumbing, but it is a file
emitted by an existing tool and read by an existing loader pattern, and it
degrades to today's behaviour when absent. The real risk is a hot list from the
wrong workload being applied silently — hence the match-rate log line, which is
not optional.

**Highest:** the retention band (step 7). It changes which candidates reach
final measurement, so a bad band shows up as worse winners rather than as an
error. Gate it behind the canary as originally planned: land step 4 first so
every family has a calibration to derive the band from, otherwise most
signatures fall to the dispersion fallback and the band is derived from a
three-sample MAD, which is barely better than a constant.

Measured context for expectations: uniform 15-sample screening changed the
aggregate by 0.2 points, within run-to-run variation. **This work should be
justified as buying confidence in per-signature results and protection against
silent mis-measurement, not as a performance gain.**

The impact budget is the exception to that framing — it is a *tuning time*
saving and a reallocation, and it should be measured as such: same call-weighted
result, less wall clock, with the top decile measured more thoroughly than
before rather than less.

7.4 (hot-signature ranking) is the basis for the impact budget, and this sharpens it: rank by time contribution rather than frequency. 12.4 (hard eligibility before launch) is unaffected. The screening retention policy currently documented in HI12 ("native always + top 3 by median + everything within 10% of best") is what this supersedes.

## Notes

Proposed by the owner after RV21 established that a three-sample median carries roughly 14% error while the retention band was 10%.
