# Measurement fidelity — adaptive launch batching and the cache-residency question

## Description

HI12 and HI24 are about how measurements are *compared*. This item is about what they *mean*, which is a separate question and currently an unexamined one.

Two issues, both in `time_candidate` (`hip-autotune-tuner.cu:160-212`), both affecting every number the project has ever produced.

**`launches_per_sample` is fixed at 1 and was designed not to be.** Its own comment says it is "raised for kernels below event noise" (`hip-autotune-tuner.cuh:56`) and nothing anywhere raises it. Every sample therefore carries one hipEvent pair's worth of fixed cost, which is a constant added equally to every candidate — and a constant added to both sides of a ratio compresses that ratio toward 1. On the small signatures that constant is a material fraction of the reading: `TUNING-DETAIL.md` has winners at **12.36 us** and **12.56 us** with neighbours ranked 1.6% and 2.9% behind. Those margins are being decided at a scale where the harness's own floor is not negligible.

**Back-to-back launches measure a hot cache.** Warmup plus every sample launches the same kernel, on the same stream, over the same operands. By sample time src0 is L2-resident to whatever extent it fits. In production the matmul runs once, between other operations, on a cache holding something else. The bias is not uniform across candidates — it depends on each candidate's access pattern and working set — so it can reorder a ranking rather than merely shifting it.

The second issue is stated as a question rather than a fix on purpose. The cheap experiment that answers it should run before any harness change is designed.

## Steps

1. **Adaptive `launches_per_sample`, calibrated from native.** After native's screening measurement and before any candidate is screened, compute the batch size once for the signature and apply it identically to every candidate.
2. **Record it.** `launches_per_sample` goes in the per-result JSONL record. A measurement whose batch size is unknown cannot be compared against one from another run.
3. **Add `GGML_HIP_TUNE_FLUSH_L2`** (default 0): between samples, never inside the timed window, evict L2 by writing a buffer larger than it.
4. **Run the residency experiment** on the ten highest-impact signatures from `TUNING-DETAIL.md`, with and without the flush, at `launches_per_sample = 1`.
5. **Decide from the result**, and record the decision either way. If rankings do not move, close the question in `COVERAGE_AUDIT.md` and leave the flush as a diagnostic knob. If they do move, the default and probably the whole retention policy need revisiting, and that is a new item rather than a quiet flag flip.
6. **Re-baseline** whatever HI35's impact model reported before the change, since batch size changes every median it consumes.

## Detailed Solution & Technical Design

### Adaptive launch batching

#### The rule

```
launches_per_sample = clamp(ceil(min_sample_us / native_median_us), 1, max_lps)
```

defaults `min_sample_us = 100`, `max_lps = 32`.

#### Three properties that matter more than the formula

**Calibrated from native, not per candidate.** If each candidate chose its own
batch size, candidates would be compared across different amounts of amortised
fixed cost, which is a worse error than the one being fixed. Native is measured
first (`hip-autotune-tuner.cu:367-392`), so its median is available before any
candidate is screened, and one value covers the signature.

**Recorded, not implicit.** Two runs of the same signature at different batch
sizes produce different absolute medians. Without the value in the artifact,
HI23's cross-release comparison and HI35's impact model are both comparing
numbers taken under different conditions with no way to tell.

**Bounded above.** `max_lps = 32` because a batch is also an amortisation of
everything else that varies per launch, and past some point the measurement
stops describing a single call in a real graph. 32 x a 12 us kernel is ~390 us
per sample, which is already far above any plausible event-timing floor.

#### Expected effect, as a prediction

On `f099775dd9793993` (12.36 us) the batch becomes 8; on `7bedae49d322d5d7`
(12.56 us) 8; on `021751e78d1d49e0` (42.32 us) 3; on `615f6214f9597bef`
(766.85 us) it stays 1. So the change touches exactly the MMVQ draft-width
signatures and leaves the large MMQ ones alone.

If batching is doing what it should, the *spread* between neighbouring MMVQ
candidates should **widen** rather than narrow — the compression is being
removed. A ranking that becomes flatter after batching means the batch is
introducing an effect of its own, most likely cache residency, which is the
other half of this item.

### The cache-residency question

#### Why it is a question and not yet a defect

The bias is real but its magnitude is bounded by whether the working set fits.
gfx1100 has 6 MB of L2 (RX 7900 GRE / XTX, Infinity Cache is separate and much
larger). Against the recorded shapes:

| Signature | src0 | q8_0 bytes | vs 6 MB L2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `d4d829f52511ebc2` | 5120 x 24 | ~131 KB | fully resident |
| `f099775dd9793993` | 5120 x 24 | ~131 KB | fully resident |
| `157e7b98d6b14460` | 5120 x 5120 | ~28 MB | 4.6x over |
| `20a1ffdbaedfc690` | 5120 x 8704 | ~47 MB | 8x over |

That is a sharp, falsifiable prediction: **the residency bias should be visible
on the small-src0 signatures and absent on the large ones.** If a flush changes
rankings on `20a1ffdbaedfc690`, the explanation is not L2 and the experiment has
found something else. If it changes nothing anywhere, the concern is closed for
the cost of two tuning runs.

The signatures at the top of the impact ranking are mostly the large ones, so
the most likely outcome is that this matters for a minority of the workload —
but `d4d829f52511ebc2` is 13,440 calls with a **+69.76%** recorded winner, the
second-largest margin in the whole table, and it is fully L2-resident. A margin
that large resting on an untested measurement assumption is worth two runs.

#### The flush

A trivial kernel writing a buffer of `2 x hipDeviceAttributeL2CacheSize`,
launched between samples and outside the event window. It must not be inside the
window, or it is being timed; it must not be between the launches *within* a
batch, which is why the experiment pins `launches_per_sample = 1`. Those two
constraints mean the flush and batching are mutually exclusive by construction,
which is worth stating in the code rather than discovering later.

#### What the experiment cannot settle

Even a flushed measurement is not production: in production the cache holds the
*previous operation's* data, not nothing. Flushing measures the cold extreme,
back-to-back measures the hot extreme, and the truth is between them and depends
on the graph. So the experiment's output is a bound on the size of the error,
not a corrected number. If the bound is small, the question is closed; if it is
large, the right response is probably to measure candidates inside a
representative op sequence, which is a much larger piece of work and belongs in
its own item.

### Two findings that change the design before any code is written

**The four call sites are at `:579-581`, `:609-611`, `:685-686` and `:788-789`,**
not the `:372/:402/:474/:553` the item was drafted against. The fourth is inside
the HI24 canary's interleaved re-measure, which is easy to miss and is exactly
where a missed thread-through would produce a canary that compares a batched
measurement against an unbatched one and fires on its own inconsistency.

**Calibration has a chicken-and-egg the item does not address.** The rule needs
`native_median_us`; native's median comes from the screening measurement at
`:579-581`, which itself takes a `launches_per_sample`. Measuring native at 1 and
every candidate at 8 is worse than measuring everything at 1: the screening
retention band would then compare native's unamortised median against
candidates' amortised ones, and native is the baseline the whole signature is
judged against. Three ways out:

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Calibrate from `est_bytes` or the shape, no probe | No GPU cost, but it is a guess about a quantity that is cheap to measure. The whole item is about stopping guessing at this scale. |
| Measure native once at the chosen batch, accept the mismatch for screening | Cheapest, and wrong in the one place it matters. |
| **Probe, calibrate, re-measure native** | One extra short measurement per signature. Recommended. |

The probe is `warmup_launches` plus 3 samples at `lps = 1`, which is already the
cost of one screening entry. Below is that ordering.

### Calibration, once per signature

```cpp
// Fixed per-sample cost (two event records, one sync) is added equally to every
// candidate, and a constant added to both sides of a ratio pulls that ratio
// toward 1. At the 12 us draft-width signatures in TUNING-DETAIL.md the ranking
// separates neighbours by 1.6%, which is the scale that cost operates on.
//
// Calibrated from native and applied to every candidate in this signature:
// per-candidate batch sizes would compare candidates across different amounts
// of amortised overhead, which is a worse error than the one being fixed.
int launches_per_sample_for(double native_median_us,
                            const ggml_hip_tuner_config & config) {
    if (native_median_us <= 0.0 || config.min_sample_us <= 0.0) {
        return 1;
    }
    const double needed = std::ceil(config.min_sample_us / native_median_us);
    return (int) std::clamp(needed, 1.0, (double) config.max_launches_per_sample);
}
```

```python
def _ensure_column(connection, table: str, column: str, decl: str) -> None:
    """Additive migration for a database created by an earlier schema.

    `dispatch-db.sql` is CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS throughout, so re-running
    it against an existing database is a no-op and a new column never appears.
    Every widening of a table needs one of these or the next INSERT fails with
    `no such column` on exactly the databases people already have.
    """
    existing = {
        row[1] for row in connection.execute(f"PRAGMA table_info({table})")
    }
    if column not in existing:
        connection.execute(f"ALTER TABLE {table} ADD COLUMN {column} {decl}")


_ensure_column(connection, "measurement", "launches_per_sample",
               "INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1")
```

of that result, so it is read once per result line and bound in the existing
measurement `INSERT`.

### The flush — and the cache it does not actually flush

The item sizes the buffer at `2 x hipDeviceAttributeL2CacheSize`. On the target
hardware that is **wrong by an order of magnitude**, and wrong in the direction
that produces a confident null result:

> gfx1100 is Navi 31. It has 6 MB of L2 **and** 64-96 MB of Infinity Cache
> (MALL) behind it, depending on the SKU. `hipDeviceAttributeL2CacheSize`
> reports the 6 MB and knows nothing about the MALL. A 12 MB eviction buffer
> leaves a 5120x24 src0 (~131 KB) resident in Infinity Cache, so the flushed arm
> and the unflushed arm read from the same place and the experiment reports "no
> effect" for a reason that has nothing to do with the hypothesis.

Size against the last level, not the first, and make it explicit rather than
derived from an attribute that does not describe it:

```cpp
// Sized to evict the *last* level, which on Navi 31 is 64-96 MB of Infinity
// Cache sitting behind a 6 MB L2. hipDeviceAttributeL2CacheSize reports the L2
// only, so deriving the buffer from it would size a 12 MB flush against a 96 MB
// cache and produce a null result by construction.
//
// 256 MB by default: comfortably past the largest MALL on this generation, and
// a rounding error against the GPU memory a tuning run already holds.
size_t evict_bytes() {
    static const size_t bytes = [] {
        if (const char * v = getenv("GGML_HIP_TUNE_FLUSH_MB")) {
            return (size_t) atoll(v) << 20;
        }
        return (size_t) 256 << 20;
    }();
    return bytes;
}

// `volatile` on the store, so the write is not elided: a kernel whose only
// effect is dead stores is a kernel that flushes nothing, and it fails silently.
__global__ void ggml_hip_l2_evict(char * __restrict__ buffer, size_t n) {
    const size_t stride = (size_t) gridDim.x * blockDim.x;
    for (size_t i = blockIdx.x * (size_t) blockDim.x + threadIdx.x;
         i < n; i += stride) {
        buffer[i] = (char) (i & 0xff);
    }
}
```

Allocated once for the process rather than per signature — 256 MB through
`ctx.pool()` per signature would churn the allocator harder than the thing being
measured:

```cpp
char * evict_buffer() {
    static char * buffer = [] {
        char * p = nullptr;
        if (hipMalloc((void **) &p, evict_bytes()) != hipSuccess) {
            GGML_LOG_WARN("bigcherry: cannot allocate %zu MB for the L2 flush; "
                          "GGML_HIP_TUNE_FLUSH_L2 disabled\n",
                          evict_bytes() >> 20);
            return (char *) nullptr;
        }
        return p;
    }();
    return buffer;
}
```

`time_candidate` gains a `bool flush_l2` parameter rather than reading the
config: it is called from four places, one of which (the canary re-measure) must
be able to match whatever the main measurement did, and threading it explicitly
makes a mismatch a compile error rather than a silent inconsistency.

**Verify the flush does something before believing any result it produces.** A
flushed run's *native* median on a small-src0 signature must be measurably higher
than an unflushed one. If native is unchanged, the buffer is too small or the
stores were elided, and both arms of the experiment measured the same thing.

### `tools/bigcherry/compare_tunes.py`

Needed here, and again by HI35 and HI36; none of them should answer "did this
change the answer" by eye. Stdlib only — there is no numpy or scipy anywhere in
`tools/`, so the rank correlation is written out:

```python
def spearman(a: list[float], b: list[float]) -> float | None:
    """Rank correlation, with midranks for ties.

    Written out rather than imported: `tools/` has no third-party dependency
    and acquiring one for a fifteen-line function would make the analysis
    harder to run than the tuning it analyses.

    Ties matter here and are not rare -- a signature where four candidates
    were rejected before timing has four zero medians, and naive ranking would
    order them arbitrarily and report a correlation that is an artifact of the
    sort.
    """
    n = len(a)
    if n < 2 or n != len(b):
        return None
    ra, rb = _midranks(a), _midranks(b)
    mean_a = sum(ra) / n
    mean_b = sum(rb) / n
    cov = sum((x - mean_a) * (y - mean_b) for x, y in zip(ra, rb))
    var_a = sum((x - mean_a) ** 2 for x in ra)
    var_b = sum((y - mean_b) ** 2 for y in rb)
    if var_a <= 0 or var_b <= 0:
        return None            # a constant column has no ordering to correlate
    return cov / math.sqrt(var_a * var_b)


def _midranks(values: list[float]) -> list[float]:
    order = sorted(range(len(values)), key=lambda i: values[i])
    ranks = [0.0] * len(values)
    i = 0
    while i < len(order):
        j = i
        while j + 1 < len(order) and values[order[j + 1]] == values[order[i]]:
            j += 1
        shared = (i + j) / 2.0 + 1.0
        for k in range(i, j + 1):
            ranks[order[k]] = shared
        i = j + 1
    return ranks


def compare(before: Path, after: Path) -> list[dict[str, Any]]:
    """Per-signature: did the winner change, and did the ordering behind it?

    Keyed on the *signature* digest. The dispatch digest mixes in the manifest
    hash and source revision (standards 13.1), so two tunes of the same shapes
    from different builds share no dispatch digests at all and an inner join on
    it silently returns nothing -- which reads exactly like "no signatures in
    common" rather than like a bug.

    A winner can survive while the ordering behind it churns, and the churn is
    the earlier warning, so both are reported.
    """
    lhs = {r["signature"]: r for r in read_results(before) if r.get("signature")}
    rhs = {r["signature"]: r for r in read_results(after) if r.get("signature")}

    rows = []
    for signature in sorted(set(lhs) & set(rhs)):
        a, b = lhs[signature], rhs[signature]
        # Only candidates measured in *both* runs can be correlated; a
        # candidate rejected in one arm has no ordering to compare.
        ma = {c["name"]: c["median_us"] for c in a["candidates"]
              if c.get("status") == "ok" and c.get("median_us")}
        mb = {c["name"]: c["median_us"] for c in b["candidates"]
              if c.get("status") == "ok" and c.get("median_us")}
        shared = sorted(set(ma) & set(mb))
        rows.append(
            {
                "signature": signature,
                "winner_before": a.get("winner", ""),
                "winner_after": b.get("winner", ""),
                "winner_changed": a.get("winner") != b.get("winner"),
                "margin_before": a.get("improvement_pct", 0.0),
                "margin_after": b.get("improvement_pct", 0.0),
                "shared_candidates": len(shared),
                "spearman": spearman([ma[n] for n in shared],
                                     [mb[n] for n in shared]),
                "lps_before": a.get("launches_per_sample", 1),
                "lps_after": b.get("launches_per_sample", 1),
            }
        )
    return rows
```

  GGML_HIP_DISPATCH_MODE=tune \

- Every candidate within one signature reports the same value. A per-candidate
  difference is the failure this design exists to prevent.
- Absolute medians on the large signatures move by less than 1%. They should be
  untouched — the batch size did not change for them.
- On the small signatures, the spread between adjacent candidates **widens**.
  If it narrows, batching has introduced an effect rather than removed one, and
  the residency experiment is the first place to look.
- `test-backend-ops -o MUL_MAT`: 0 failures, `2/2 backends passed`, unchanged.
- Sweep wall time grows by no more than the batch factor on the affected
  signatures — check it, since a mis-clamped `max_lps` would be invisible except
  as a slow sweep.

### Residency experiment

- Winners on `20a1ffdbaedfc690` and `157e7b98d6b14460` (src0 4.6x and 8x over
  L2) are **unchanged** by the flush. If they move, L2 is not the mechanism and
  the experiment's premise is wrong.
- `d4d829f52511ebc2` and `f099775dd9793993` (src0 ~131 KB, fully resident) are
  where any effect must appear if the hypothesis holds.
- Rank correlation is reported per signature, not only winner identity — a
  winner can survive while the ordering behind it churns, and the churn is the
  earlier warning.
- The flush is verifiably doing something: a flushed run's *native* median on a
  small-src0 signature is measurably higher than an unflushed one. If native is
  unchanged, the buffer is too small or the kernel is being elided.
- The decision is written down in `COVERAGE_AUDIT.md` either way. A null result
  is the valuable outcome here and must not be left as an untracked memory.

## Effort & Risk

M. The batching change is small and mechanical; its risk is that it silently
re-bases every absolute timing in the project, so it must land with the JSONL
field and the schema column in the same change, not after.

The experiment is two tuning runs plus a comparison tool, and the tool is
reusable — HI35 and HI36 both need "did this change the answer" and none of them
should answer it by eye.

**Sequencing.** After HI12's E-series, so there is a stable decision rule to
evaluate against; and after HI24's hot list, so the experiment can target the
ten signatures that matter instead of sweeping 1151. Before HI35's impact model
is quoted anywhere, since batching moves the medians that model multiplies.

**The honest risk** is that the residency experiment comes back positive on a
high-margin signature — `d4d829f52511ebc2` at +69.76% is fully L2-resident and
is exactly the profile that would show it. That would put a real question mark
over a subset of recorded winners. Better to find it deliberately for the cost
of two runs than to keep quoting the margin.
